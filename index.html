01 — System

A ramp read from the specimen.

Fold the 55 distinct glyphs into 35 case classes and sort them. Fourteen adjacent pairs survive as hexadecimal, and they run 0x01 to 0xEF without a break. The fifteenth candidate is FG, which is not a number. The run stops there.

Fourteen steps.
One warm exception.

Each pair, repeated three times, is a colour: #010101, #121212 … #EFEFEF. Fourteen neutral greys, and not one of them picked by eye. Surfaces, hairlines, captions and display type all come out of that list.

The exception is #E04A2B. It is reserved for anomalies — the digit that never appears, and the two letters that appear twice. Nothing else earns it.

04 — The absent five

All twenty-six letters are in here. Nine of the ten digits are. Five is not.

a.Name it early

A brief always arrives with a hole in it: the constraint nobody wrote down, the decision with no owner, the number everyone is quietly avoiding. My first two weeks are mostly spent finding the hole and saying it out loud, while it is still cheap to fix.

b.Build for the gap

Systems fail at the edges they pretend not to have. So the empty state gets designed before the happy path, the loading state gets a rhythm, and the number that can never be known gets a typographic form instead of a zero.

c.Keep one warm pigment

Restraint only reads as restraint if there is something held back. One colour, used maybe nine times on a page, is worth more than a palette of forty used everywhere. The discipline is the design.

05 — Ledger

What the specimen actually contains.

Computed in the browser from the raw string, not typed in by hand. If any figure here disagreed with the source, the layout would be lying.

A 128-character specimen, 8 chapters of 16. Thirteen even digits, thirteen odd — digital symmetry, arrived at by accident. Fifty-five distinct glyphs, which fold to thirty-five.

Two glyphs genuinely repeat — vv at 4 and ll at 104. A third, nN at 74, only looks like a repeat. Six letters appear in one case only. The rest are singletons.

06 — Practice

Eleven years of making software that has to be believed.

Independent since 2021. Before that, in-house on design systems and data products. Based in Lisbon, working across European and US time zones.

I work between design and engineering, which mostly means I am the person who has to make the argument and the thing that proves it.

My background is type and editorial layout; my day job is React, TypeScript, tokens and motion. That combination is unfashionable and useful: I can take a page from a rough sketch to a shipped component library without the idea degrading at the handover, because there is no handover.

Recent collaborators include a water authority in Rotterdam, a carbon accounting platform in Oslo, and an independent record label that wanted covers generated from the audio. All three needed interfaces that could hold uncertain numbers without pretending otherwise.
